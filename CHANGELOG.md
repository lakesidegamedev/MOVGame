# Changelog

All notable changes to Margin of Victory are recorded here. The format follows
[Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/).

The game has stayed at version 0.1.0 since it began, so the history below is grouped by month.
Once releases carry their own version numbers, each release gets its own heading.

Every pull request that changes the game, the apps, content or tooling adds its entry under
**Unreleased** in the same pull request. A release moves those entries under a new heading.

### Changed

- Android locks paid elections you do not own, like iPhone and the web, but only once Google Play
  can sell that election's pack. A locked election shows a lock and Unlock in the library, the
  briefing and the U.S. setup, and Unlock opens its pack in the Store. Free elections, the daily
  challenge and anything you own through your Lakeside account, Google Play, the App Store, the
  Complete Collection, the Global Elections Pack or the Supporter perk always open.
- iPhone now follows the same rule as Android: a paid election locks only once the App Store
  can sell that election's own pack, so a new country pack that is not on sale there yet stays
  playable. Elections unlocked on your account with a code always open on iPhone, also when
  the account is not linked to Lakeside, and count under Owned in the library.
- Every election now has its own issues, named the way people talked about them that year: the
  Depression and relief in 1932, the war in Europe in 1940, Korea and communism in 1952, the
  miners' strike in February 1974, the cost of unity in Germany in 1990, free trade in Canada in
  1988, and so on through all 121 elections. COVID no longer turns up before 2020, and Germany's
  older elections no longer show issues from 2025. The issue pickers, the salience panels, the
  Ask handoff and the apps all use the election's own list; an issue the election did not have
  can no longer be raised by an ad, an event or a pivot. Custom US races use the issues of the
  nearest earlier election, and Pandemic Mode adds a pandemic issue to any year. Saved campaigns
  keep loading.
- Canada's Bloc Québécois and India's state parties (Trinamool, the Samajwadi Party, the BJD,
  the YSRCP and the AIADMK) now play as regional parties, like Spain's Catalan and Basque
  parties: the setup shows where they stand and why they cannot win a majority, the results
  score them against their real seat count, and generic campaign events no longer target them
  with headlines written for national parties.
- Difficulty now means the same thing in every country. Normal gives you a stronger campaign
  than your rivals (your campaigning lands harder, plus one extra action and a little more money
  each week), so a well-run campaign can win a close election from either side, while a
  historical landslide stays out of reach. Easy now gives a head start sized to the race: almost
  nothing in a near-tie, a big lift in a landslide, always leaving the last stretch for your
  campaign to win. Hard makes your campaigning land a little lighter, and outside the United
  States only your main rival campaigns harder, so smaller parties no longer split your rival's
  vote for you and make Hard easier than Normal. Every election's difficulty rating was measured
  again and updated.
- Platform, Debate Prep and Issue Pivot reach the whole country for free, so repeating them all
  week was stronger than any other plan. The second one in a week now has half the effect, the
  third a quarter, and so on.
- iPhone and iPad: the campaign library lists all 14 countries and 121 elections by country, with
  search, Free and Owned filters, challenge and era filters, and a briefing for each election that
  says how it is won before you pick a side. India's nine sides, Spain's regional parties and every
  other party list fit on a small phone and with large text.
- iPhone and iPad: the store lists every pack from the catalog with the Complete Collection first,
  says what you own and why, marks each country's free elections, shows the App Store price when a
  pack is on sale and "Coming soon" when it is not, and keeps Restore purchases.
- iPhone and iPad: the rules read correctly for every election. Mexico says most points wins, the
  United States shows that year's electoral college (266 of 531 to win from 1932 to 1956) on setup,
  the desk and results, states that cast no electoral votes have no tile, election night names the
  real candidates and parties, and a regional party (Spain, Canada's Bloc, India's state parties)
  is told where it stands and is measured against its real seat count.
- iPhone and iPad: election setup describes the new difficulty ladder under the Easy, Normal and
  Hard picker, for the United States and every other country.
- iPhone: at large text sizes the United States campaign desk no longer cuts off your cash and the
  election name.
- iPhone and iPad: the United States week planner lists that election's own issues under the names
  voters used that year, so a 1932 campaign argues over the Depression and never over COVID.
- Spain: the Catalan and Basque parties are now playable as regional parties in every election
  they stood in: ERC, CiU and its successors Democràcia i Llibertat and Junts, the PNV, and
  Amaiur and EH Bildu, each with its real lead candidate. They stand only in Catalonia or in the
  Basque Country and Navarre, so they cannot win a majority; the setup says so and the results
  screen measures you against the party's real seat count. Two new story beats cover the Catalan
  lists of 2015 and the PNV's record before April 2019.
- Every country now has a free election, playable without an account: the newest election of
  each country (France's 2022 runoff rather than the speculative 2027 one), plus the US 2024 and
  2020 races as before. The free election stays free when newer elections arrive. Country packs
  hold the rest of each country's elections; the Store shows the free one on the pack page as
  "Free", and the base game section lists every free election by country. Nobody loses anything:
  owners of a pack or the retired Global pack keep every election they had.
- Android: a full interface pass. The app now uses the same colors, type sizes, spacing and
  party color rules as the web game, so dark party colors stay readable and every screen has
  one clear main action. The campaign desk for the U.S., the UK and every other country shows a
  seat bar with the majority line, a standings table, a tappable map with a matching region
  list, and End week pinned to the bottom of the screen. Long party lists, such as India's ten
  playable sides, are picked from a full list instead of a dropdown.
- Android: the campaign library groups every country and its elections,
  with search and filters for free and owned elections, difficulty and era. Each election opens
  a briefing with the party picker, difficulty and seed.
- Android: the Store lists every pack, with the Complete Collection first. It shows what you
  own and why (bought, included in the Complete Collection, or part of an older Global pack),
  the Google Play price when a pack is on sale, and "Coming soon" when it is not. There is no
  Buy button for a pack you cannot buy yet.
- Android: results, election night, saved campaigns, leaderboards, settings and the account
  screen use the new layout, with numbers in aligned columns and larger touch targets.

- Countries are now self-contained add-ons inside the game. Each country carries its own
  elections, store pack, daily challenge sides and "play next" links, and the web game, the
  iPhone app and the Android app all list countries and packs from that one source. Nothing
  changes for players: every election, pack, save and leaderboard entry keeps its id.
- The Complete Collection automatically includes every paid election of every country, including
  countries added later. The retired Global pack keeps exactly the fourteen Canada, Germany,
  France and Australia elections it always had.
- The daily challenge now includes newly added countries on a fair share of days. Every day that
  was already assigned to one of the original elections keeps it, and past leaderboards keep
  showing the election that was played that day.
- The web and desktop game has one consistent look: a shared set of colors, type sizes,
  spacing, buttons, cards, tabs, tables and modals on every screen, with numbers in aligned
  figures instead of a typewriter font.
- The campaign desk works the same way in the US, UK and every other country: race and week
  on the left, the live seat or electoral vote bar, your resources, and Undo and End Week
  always in view. On phones Undo and End Week sit in a bar at the bottom of the screen.
- The map fills the desk and stays fully on screen. Map tiles and regions can be selected with
  the keyboard.
- The election library groups every election by country, with search, a country rail, and
  filters for owned or locked, difficulty and era. It grows with new countries on its own.
- Setup screens list elections as compact tiles with difficulty and lock state, and parties
  as cards with their colors.
- Results show the verdict, map and standings beside your next election, score and
  post-mortem on wide screens. Election night uses larger maps.
- Modals close from the same button everywhere and open as bottom sheets on phones.
- The Unlock prompt on a locked election is now the Store, opened on the right pack with the
  Complete Collection beside it. The Redeem option in the menu now opens a code-only dialog.
- Account and menu text no longer quotes prices; the Store shows the current ones.
- The button that leaves a campaign says "Home" in every country.
- The Redeem a code dialog matches the rest of the interface.
- Each United States election now uses its own Electoral College. The majority, the EV bar,
  election night, results, score, achievements and the campaign charts follow that year's
  total instead of always 270 of 538. States that cast no electoral votes that year are left
  off the map.

### Fixed

- Country flags now show as flags on Windows, in the desktop app and in every browser. Before,
  Windows drew two boxed letters (US, GB, DE) wherever a flag appeared, on the landing page,
  the library, setup, the store and the campaign desk. The game now carries a 4 KB flag font for
  the 14 countries it uses.
- A general campaign event, such as a gaffe, a newspaper endorsement or a debate win, now runs at
  most once a campaign in every country, so the same headline never comes round twice. Decision
  events such as a debate invitation can still return.
- The Complete Collection's own page and card, on the web and on Android, now count every election
  in the game and say how many of those are already free, matching the Store banner, instead of
  counting only the paid ones. Android's country packs also count their free election, as the web
  does.
- Ireland's map draws Northern Ireland as neutral land outside the game, so the island reads whole
  and nothing suggests it votes in a Dáil election. Italy's Abroad box and New Zealand's Māori seats
  box now have a dashed frame, on the web and on Android, so they read as boxes standing for voters
  rather than places.
- The campaign timeline's closest contests now read "behind by 3.2" instead of "behind -3.2".
- Two difficulty labels move after the issue and event changes, measured by a full balance run:
  US 1996 (Clinton v. Dole) and the UK's October 1974 election go from hard to medium.
- History check across the new countries and the older Canada, Australia, Germany, France and US
  elections: every national seat total and chamber size was checked against published results and
  held. A few regional vote shares in Canada, Australia and the US were corrected, and France's
  1981 and 1988 runoff regions now use the official results instead of modelled shares. Story
  beats that were misdated, misattributed or could not be sourced were corrected or replaced (for
  example the German Greens' 1990 leaders, the 1993 Campbell line in Canada, and the 1944 US
  campaign's Pearl Harbor wording), and unverifiable quotes were removed.
- Landing page: the opening paragraph no longer says "across 14 countries" twice, the country
  bundle cards line up their prices and buttons, and the Complete Collection card counts every
  election.
- Store: the country pack tiles show their cover photo, flag and country name again (they had
  collapsed to text only). The free base game lists one election per cell, so the two free US
  elections no longer stretch a row, and on phones each free election is one line with its
  flag, difficulty and Play. The Complete Collection figures stay on one row on phones.
- A campaign no longer shows the same debate win twice ("X's candidate wins the debate" could
  fire in week 1 and again in week 2). The leaders' debate win is now a once-per-campaign beat
  in every country, and Mexico's campaigns draw from four more generic events.
- The results gauge now says which way a seat or point margin goes ("37 behind", "12 short",
  "5 over") instead of printing the size alone, and "Why you lost" says "5.1 points behind"
  rather than "-5.1 points against".
- Issue descriptions shared by every election of a country no longer describe only the newest
  one: Germany 1990 no longer mentions the war in Ukraine, the UK no longer offers "Brexit" in
  1951, and Canada, Australia, France, Italy, Spain, Ireland and New Zealand read correctly in
  every year they cover.
- Store and landing copy now matches every election in each pack: the United States pack says
  24 races from 1932 to 2024, Canada says 282 to 343 seats and Australia 148 to 151. Mexico's
  left is now named "PRD/Morena left".
- Geographic maps now show every region's name at phone and desktop sizes. Small or crowded
  regions are labelled in the sea next to them (Prince Edward Island and Nova Scotia, Auckland,
  Kerala and Bengal, the three Dublin regions, central Japan, Veneto and Apulia, Espírito Santo
  and Rio, the centre of Mexico), and a region's name is always placed before any neighbour's
  seat count, so a seat count never hides a name. Japan's blocks use short labels ("Hok",
  "N Kan", "Kinki"), as do Ireland's commuter belt and Dublin regions, Abruzzo-Molise and
  Mexico's Yucatán; the full name is still on hover and in the region panel.
- France, Brazil and Mexico: presidency points now follow the vote. Before, points barely moved
  until a region swung hard, so a candidate could win the runoff vote 53 to 47 and still lose
  on points. Points are now the national vote split, and the map shows each region's share.
- A rival's contrast broadcast or dossier against you was strengthened by your own difficulty
  bonus, and yours against them weakened by theirs. Attacks now use the attacker's setting.
- On Easy, a party that stands in only part of a country (the Bloc Québécois, India's regional
  parties, Spain's Catalan and Basque parties) no longer appears on the ballot in regions where it
  never stood. The Easy head start now applies only where your party is actually running.
- Parties with very dark colors, such as the CDU/CSU in Germany, are now readable on maps,
  seat bars, standings and setup cards.
- Small text and party-colored names now meet contrast guidelines on the dark panels.
- Desktop apps now reach the game's servers (they were calling their own address).
- Purchases made on another Lakeside app no longer stop the Store from loading what you own
  when that app sells a pack this version does not have yet.
- A pack the web store cannot sell yet shows as "Coming soon" instead of a Buy button.
- "Play next" after a result now runs through every election of the country in order, so it no
  longer skips Canada 2015 and 2019, Germany 2017, Australia 2019 or the US elections before 2000.
- After a hung parliament the results headline is colored by the party it names, not by yours.
- The landing page, FAQ, pricing and page description now count countries, elections and voting
  systems from the game itself, so they no longer say "6 countries, 6 voting systems", and
  the FAQ quotes the current store prices.
- The France-only "six planning weeks" note no longer shows on Brazil's setup screen.
- The election library shows a one-line summary for every country, not only the first six.
- Region names on the geographic maps no longer pile on top of each other in crowded areas such
  as Tokyo, Delhi, the Basque Country, Auckland or London. Labels keep a readable size on phones
  and stay inside the map; a region with no room for its label keeps its color and shows its
  name on hover.
- Seat bars only print a party's name when it fits its slice, so small parties no longer show
  cut-off names, and the majority marker no longer cuts through party names.
- Every country appears in the library's country filter on wide screens instead of running off
  the edge.
- The results card's next-election map is visible for parties with dark colors, and the
  campaign summary shows each country's own currency.
- On phones the setup screen's back and start buttons no longer wrap awkwardly with long party
  names.
- 1960 now has its real 537 electoral votes and 269 to win: the District of Columbia no longer
  votes (its first presidential election was 1964), and Maine and Nebraska carry their 1960
  votes of 5 and 6.
